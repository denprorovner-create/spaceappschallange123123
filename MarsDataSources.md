# Hellas Survey data and physics

## Landing site

The target is **32.91015625° S, 62.19921875° E**, at **−8,208 m** relative to the MOLA GMM3 areoid. It is the lowest median cell identified by the northern Hellas scan and matches the global minimum reported in the 128 pixels/degree MOLA product label. The 4 pixels/degree global raster independently shows its lowest cell in the same region. The 128 pixels/degree target cell has one contributing observation. The centered finite-difference slope is about **0.8°** and the nearby 11 × 11 cells contain slopes up to **11.6°**. This is a regional terrain comparison. It cannot resolve rover-scale rocks or certify a landing ellipse.

- **Topography:** NASA Mars Global Surveyor Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MGS/MOLA), PDS collection MGS-M-MOLA-5-MEGDR-L3-V1.0. `hellas-mola-128ppd.img` is a 512 × 384 crop of `MEGT00N000HB.IMG`, covering 60–64° E and 31–34° S. Samples are big-endian signed 16-bit median elevations in meters. Original sample at zero-based row 4212, column 7961. `mars-mola-global-4ppd.img` is `MEGT90N000CB.IMG`, used when a simulated landing falls outside the crop. [PDS product index](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/mgs/mgs-m-mola-5-megdr-l3-v1/mgsl_300x/meg128/) · [Data specification](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/mgs/mgs-m-mola-5-megdr-l3-v1/mgsl_300x/document/egdrsis.htm)
- **Orbital imagery:** NASA Mars Trek Viking VIS color mosaic, `Mars_Viking_MDIM21_ClrMosaic_global_232m`, level 7 row 87 column 172. The bundled tile covers 61.875–63.28125° E and 32.34375–33.75° S. It appears in the mission brief and satellite view and textures the regional MOLA mesh. The approach globe uses the JPL/USGS global Viking texture at a radius of 3,389.5 km. [NASA Mars Trek API](https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=mars) · [JPL global Mars texture](https://space.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/mars.html)
- **Atmospheric reference:** NASA's Mars Fact Sheet gives 636 Pa at the mean radius and an 11 km representative scale height. At −8,208 m the simple hydrostatic scaling gives about **1,340 Pa**. The fact sheet notes strong seasonal variation at the reference radius. The estimate is a scenario parameter. A nearby measured pressure record or a global time-dependent pressure maximum cannot be derived from MOLA. [NASA Mars Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html)

## Computed flight

The simulation loads the JPL Horizons geometric position and velocity snapshot at **2026-11-14 00:00 TDB**, J2000 ecliptic frame with the solar system barycenter as origin. The Sun, eight planets and Moon mutually gravitate. Cruise builds an interpolated ephemeris from this numerical integration and integrates the spacecraft with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method as a test particle. The coarse departure estimate seeds numerical targeting. The forecast and the spacecraft then use the same gravitational force model, including Earth and Mars, with tighter steps near each planet. Auto and Manual use the same propagator. Assisted departure and player corrections are impulsive rocket-equation burns that consume fuel. Clock warp calls the propagator; it does not play an animation over a fixed line. [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) · [JPL gravitational parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html)

At the 125 km atmospheric interface, the actual arrived state is transformed into Mars-rotating vertical and horizontal speeds. EDL initially models a vertical/downrange trajectory over spherical Mars and adds lateral thrust in powered descent. Hydrostatic integration of a representative layered CO₂ atmosphere gives static pressure and density. Drag is proportional to dynamic pressure and the deployed cross section. Thermal loads use a simplified Sutton–Graves stagnation-point estimate. Terrain altitude affects when the vehicle reaches ground. Late or steep entries, undersized parachutes, lack of fuel or early heat-shield release can end the attempt. The model uses an approximation to the temperature profile, terrain and aeroshell limits. It is not a Mars-GRAM implementation or a mission-critical forecast. [Mars 2020 post-flight atmosphere study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024612) · [NASA convective heating reference](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080013535/downloads/20080013535.pdf) · [NASA Mars landing sequence](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/final-minutes-of-curiositys-arrival-at-mars/)

## Surface operations

The simulator projects integrated descent downrange and crossrange onto Mars. Both views share the rover position, traverse, terrain and recorder. Regional geometry uses interpolated MOLA heights with planetary curvature and the Viking mosaic projected onto it. The global raster supplies elevations outside the regional crop. Rover-scale relief, boulders, field stations and their geological interpretations are simulated. The local relief affects traction and the marked boulders can block the rover. The weather station reports modeled air properties. The relay uses a representative 420 km circular overflight. Packet delay is spacecraft-to-Earth range divided by light speed at Mars arrival. Batteries, radio and hardware are gameplay engineering approximations. Artemis I was a reference for the earlier lunar flight sequence. Mars flight states come from the game’s numerical propagation.
