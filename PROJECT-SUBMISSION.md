# Project Details

Perilune is an interactive Windows game that puts players in charge of a space mission from vehicle assembly to science return. It includes Sandbox for custom mission planning and 16 preset missions. The showcase, Hellas Survey, starts in a 3D hangar on Earth. Players assemble a rocket, choose solid or liquid boosters, and equip a rover with a heat shield, parachute, descent engine, power source, radio, and science instruments. They can fly with guided controls or manage their own ascent, departure, and course correction burns.

The Mars transfer starts from NASA JPL Horizons position and velocity data. The game calculates gravitational forces and propagates the spacecraft under the influence of the Sun, planets, and Moon. At Mars, changing atmospheric density affects drag and heating. Once on the surface, players can drive the same rover in 3D or set waypoints on a map. NASA Viking orbital imagery and Mars Global Surveyor MOLA elevations show the Hellas region. Rover-scale terrain and observations are simulated.

The creative focus is making science return depend on the entire mission. Adding a spectrometer gives the rover another way to study rocks, while increasing payload mass, cost, power demand during observations, and the amount of data it needs to relay. The player has to balance launch thrust, fuel, the landing envelope, recorder capacity, battery charge, relay coverage, and light-time delay. A safe landing is one milestone. Hellas Survey is complete when the rover has studied two sites and relayed at least 0.20 GB to Earth.

We developed Perilune in C# using .NET Framework and Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF), including WPF's 3D rendering. A PowerShell build script compiles the game, and automated simulations verify mission outcomes. The game runs offline on a Windows PC. The goal is to give students a place to try an engineering decision, see which mission constraints it affects, and revise the design based on the result.

# Summary

Perilune is a Windows desktop game that lets students plan, build, fly, and operate a space mission. Its showcase, Hellas Survey, starts with a rocket and rover the player can assemble in a 3D hangar. After launching from Earth, the player follows a computed N-body trajectory to Mars, manages atmospheric descent, and drives a rover through the Hellas basin. The objective is to survey two sites and relay at least 0.20 GB of science data to Earth. The game uses NASA JPL Horizons orbital states, Viking imagery, and Mars Global Surveyor laser-altimeter elevations. Perilune addresses the challenge by connecting choices about budget, fuel, instruments, power, and communications across one playable mission. Students can see how a decision made in the hangar affects whether their science ever reaches Earth.
