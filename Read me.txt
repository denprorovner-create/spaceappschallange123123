PERILUNE
Standalone 64-bit Windows space mission game

Extract the full archive, then run Perilune.exe. Keep the assets directory beside
the executable. The game runs offline.

HELLAS SURVEY
Choose Mission → Hellas Survey, the first flight in the archive. The Rocket
and Rover tabs edit the same mission. Drag engines, tanks and boosters into
the bay, and detach them with Delete or ×. In the Rover tab, choose landing
and rover systems, then click or drag instruments onto the rover. The stock
configuration costs $159M against a $170M cap. The assembly budget updates
as you edit. Earth ascent, Mars departure and landing have separate fuel tanks.

Choose Launch from Earth, then Launch (or Enter) on the pad. Auto trajectory
flies ascent, plans and executes departure and integrates the coast to Mars.
Manual mode
lets you choose a launch date offset, parking altitude and transfer duration,
fly ascent, choose the burn time, trim departure and make correction burns.
The cyan trace is the computed prediction. The amber trace is flight history.

At the 125 km atmospheric interface, choose Take entry control. Telemetry
shows airspeed, Mach, static and dynamic pressure, density and heating.
F deploys the parachute, H releases the heat shield, and E starts powered
descent. A/D controls entry bank or landing steer. Up/Down changes throttle.
Landing assist starts enabled in Auto and can be toggled during flight.
After landing, M opens the Viking orbital map. Choose Traverse map to see the
local sites and set a waypoint, or drive with W/S and A/D in the 3D view.
For a demo, stop at two marked sites and press E with the panoramic camera
selected. R starts the relay downlink. Use 300× surface warp and Coast to
next relay as needed until at least 0.20 GB reaches Earth.
The recorder, power supply, relay horizon and Earth light time affect delivery.

See MarsDataSources.md for the MOLA raster products, target coordinates, NASA
atmospheric references and model limits.

BUILD
Choose Mission → Blue Marble → Accept mission. Use the part-family menu to find
engines, tanks, or boosters. Drag a part into the bay or double-click its card
to attach it to its compatible mount. Add tanks, solid or liquid booster pairs,
and choose propellant load. Click parts in 3D to select them and press Delete,
or use × in the fitted list. Ctrl+Z undoes the last design change. The Rocket
and Spacecraft tabs let you work on the launch stack and payload separately.
Fit several distinct instruments if you want to collect additional science.
Use Save and Load to exchange vehicle designs between missions.

The assembly header shows committed cost, the mission cap and remaining funds
or overage. The sidebar lists rocket, spacecraft and ground costs separately.
Hover over a part to compare its cost, mass, lift and electrical consequences.
Trade study estimates eclipse, contact time and returned science for your plan.
Remember a baseline, change the build, and compare the two designs.

LAUNCH
Click Launch in the assembly bay, then Launch or Enter on the pad to start the
countdown. W/S controls pitch, A/D adjusts heading, ↑/↓ adjusts liquid throttle,
and Z/X sets full/zero throttle. Space separates boosters, core, then payload.
Press F or Jettison fairing to split the shell and expose the spacecraft.
Jettison removes mass. Above most of the atmosphere it also avoids exposing
the payload to high aerodynamic pressure. Automatic separation can time this
for you. Staging close-ups briefly reduce time warp to show moving parts.
To release your spacecraft early, use Flight tools → Release spacecraft now.
Guided ascent on the top bar enables steering and automatic staging. Holding
W/S, A/D or the throttle keys takes manual steering back immediately. Hold
Shift for finer steering and throttle adjustments.
Flight tools also shows the suggested pitch and throttle. Guidance and staging
assists can be toggled independently. For a first practice run, turn both on
and set time warp to 20× after clearing the pad.
Readiness warnings are advisory. An engine without a tank cannot produce thrust,
and a vehicle with insufficient upward force stays on the pad. Budget and
deadline affect mission credit. Flight can continue after completion or expiry.
Pad camera tracks from a fixed position near the pad during early ascent.
M (or F9) switches between Rocket view and the navigation map. The same map
shows the assigned path, current trajectory, Moon and planets. Scroll or use
the zoom buttons to change scale. Right-click any visible body to focus it.
Earth is the starting focus. Use the focus list to jump to a body directly.

ORBIT AND SCIENCE
All 15 preset missions launch from Earth. For a Moon mission, fly to the
300 km Earth parking orbit and release the spacecraft. Open Maneuvers:
  B  Calculate and execute translunar injection at the next departure point.
  N  Coast to the planned lunar arrival, about four simulated days later.
  B  Brake into a lunar parking orbit. The science deadline starts here.
  N  Warp to the science orbit's maneuver node, then B for insertion.
The departure, capture, and insertion assists apply burns and consume
propellant. During the transfer you can coast in real time, use time warp,
or fly maneuver burns yourself. A burn away from the predicted trajectory
can move the capture point. The Moon's position at arrival is marked on the
map. If there is insufficient propellant, revise the propulsion system.

After release, press M for the 3D map. Cyan marks the assigned path and amber
marks your current path. White markers show apoapsis and periapsis when visible.
Rocket view uses a close camera measured in meters. The background globe is
rendered from the same viewpoint at the actual planetary radius and altitude.
Open Maneuvers to select prograde, retrograde, radial or normal burns, or use N
to warp to the suggested insertion point and B for assisted insertion. Manual
burns use propellant. Z ignites at full throttle and X cuts it.

Choose which instruments to arm, then select Start science (E). The panel shows
each instrument's status and stored and delivered data in GB. Transmit to Earth
sends stored data during a ground pass. For mission credit, deliver enough
qualifying data from the assigned experiment and orbit before the deadline.
Battery use and recorder size may make it useful to alternate science and radio.
Open Report during flight or after an attempt to review score, resource usage
and flight events. R toggles downlink.

SANDBOX
In Orbital flight, choose the body, experiment, flight constraints and orbit.
Use Edit flight plan in assembly to change those choices with the vehicle intact.
In Jezero rover, fit a chassis, aeroshell, parachute, descent stage, power,
radio and any combination of four instruments. The scenario starts at Mars
arrival during atmospheric descent. Landing assist is on by default. For
manual descent, F deploys the parachute, E starts powered descent, and the
arrow keys control thrust. A parachute alone will generally land too fast.
After touchdown drive with W/S and steer with A/D. Stop at a site, select an
instrument and press E. R commands downlink through a relay pass. The field
journal shows science, imagery reference and sample counts. Surface time warp
helps with relay and science waiting. The traverse and stations are simulated.

SIMULATION
Newtonian point-mass gravity. The Sun, eight planets and Moon mutually perturb
each other. The spacecraft is integrated as a test particle with differential
third-body accelerations. Map trails and orbit readouts represent instantaneous
osculating elements. Earth ascent and reentry use a simplified atmosphere and
aerodynamic loads. Engines have fixed rated performance. The lunar planner
uses an Earth-only approximation as an initial guess, then corrects it with
the full N-body model. The simulation integrates the coast continuously and
applies assisted injection and capture as instantaneous changes in velocity.
Changing reference bodies at capture leaves the inertial position and velocity
continuous except for the commanded burn. The Hellas Survey has a computed
Earth-to-Mars trajectory. The separate Jezero practice scenario starts during
descent. Hardware and budgets are fictional.
The game models simplified ground station geometry and ideal Sun-pointing arrays.
NASA images texture the orbital bodies. Launch terrain is illustrative.

C# sources and Build.ps1 are in the PeriluneDesktop workspace directory.
